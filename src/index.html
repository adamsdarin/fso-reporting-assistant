Reporting decision aid · Cleared contractors

Do I report this, to whom, by when?

A program-wide aid for the FSO of a cleared contractor under 32 CFR Part 117, SEAD 3 and DCSA ISL 2021-02. Pick who or what the event is about, pick what happened, answer the few questions that change the answer, and get the recipients, the rule’s own clock with a due date where the rule has a number, what to include and what to do next. Then write the one-page file note and add the entry to your reporting log.

Your records stay on your machine. The pages run entirely in your browser. Their security policy blocks any network connection, and they save nothing in the browser. What you type is gone when you close the tab unless you export it. The place to keep it is an Excel workbook on your own computer or company system.

How it decides

Three layers of duty, one path

  • The employee’s own reports under SEAD 3 and ISL 2021-02 go to the FSO or designee, who makes the DISS submission: foreign travel, foreign contacts, foreign interests, targeting and arrests, financial and treatment matters, the defined cryptocurrency and mental-health criteria, and concerns about another covered person.
  • The company’s reports under 32 CFR §117.8: espionage to the FBI, adverse information and suspicious contacts, status changes, changed conditions and FOCI, storage and emergencies, prime and subcontractor conditions, foreign contracts and material, loss or compromise, culpability, cyber incidents, and duplicate reviews to ISOO.
  • The questions that change the answer: DoD cognizance, the person’s eligibility category, a Government site, a cleared defense contractor and the DFARS clause, foreign government information, subcontractor status, and whether the preliminary inquiry has confirmed a loss. A “Not sure” answer leads to what to settle first, never a guess.
  • By when is the rule’s own wording. Where it has a number (five business days after return; 72 hours of discovery) the aid computes the date; where it says “promptly”, “immediately” or nothing, it says so and asks for your company standard.
  • Insider-threat situations have their own aid in the FSO ITPM Assistant; the espionage, adverse-information and suspicious-contact events here are the same reports, worded for the whole program.

How the records work

One workbook on your machine; the tools fill it

  1. Download the blank workbook once. It has a Reporting Log sheet and a File Notes sheet. Due dates and alerts are Excel formulas. Save it where your security program records belong, with access limited to the people who need it.
  2. Use a tool, then export. Each export has exactly the same columns, widths, drop-downs and formulas as the blank workbook.
  3. Copy the rows across. Select the rows in the export, copy them, and paste them into the first empty row of the same sheet in your workbook. The Reporting Log sheet also matches the FSO ITPM Assistant’s Reporting Log column for column, so rows paste between the two workbooks.
Made in your browser. Nothing is downloaded from a server.
Keep only what the program needs. Record facts, not rumor or innuendo, and no diagnoses (32 CFR §117.8(c)(1)). Use an internal case ID instead of a name; a name belongs only in the company’s protected records. Do not put these files in a personal or shared cloud folder, and do not attach them to email.
